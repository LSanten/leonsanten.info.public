# care is criminal: anarchist calisthenics  
## illegality and being marked as criminal 

#private-marble-keep-from-public 

Content from someone’s writing: 


_Anarchist calisthenics_ is, essentially, the practice of partaking in what, in other circumstances, could be twisted as "illegal activity," in order to **sharpen our sense that criminality is always at the tips of our fingers, that we can choose to exercise tiny acts of freedom, rebellion, non-compliance at every turn.**  

The more we practice this, the more it becomes muscle memory; the more we train our bodies to remember also the presence of our ancestors, and welcome their knowings, too, into our bodies. We hope eventually to be able to do this unconsciously, like a dance we've always known. So that when shit goes down, we can more easily trust ourselves and each other.

For us, criminality and freedom are one and the same. Why? It is not because those marked as criminal are free; precisely the opposite. Criminality is a marking deployed by the state upon the bodies it seeks to disappear and quiet. As such, it is often our instinct to shy away from being marked as criminal; for to take on the position of the criminal can feel like seizing the empire's sword and placing it upon your throat. But herein lies the paradox: it is true that the moment you seize the sword of empire, you become a criminal. But after that, what will occur? That depends upon all of us, and our capacity to care for each other, in bigger and bigger ways.  

Rather than fight against the makings and markings of criminality, which requires appeals to innocence, and our participation in marking radical forms of resistance with the stain of criminality; rather than playing their game, why not play our own? Why not instead embrace criminality, and prepare ourselves for what is to come?

### **care work as a base ingredient of our movement**  

We do not wish to be an authority on these matters; we do not see ourselves as leaders in any way. Instead, we hope for all of us to authorize ourselves to do what must be done. Which is always, first, to care for one another, both in the ways we always have done, and also in those ways we have yet to conceive of.
